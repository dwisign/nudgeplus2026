Our proprietary method
KALIBER

KALIBER is Nudgeplus's 7-step Behavioural Change Method — developed over 6+ years of field work across Indonesia. Every engagement we deliver is structured around KALIBER. It is not a theory. It is an operational protocol grounded in 60+ years of peer-reviewed behavioural science, calibrated for the Indonesian context.

K Key Behaviour — Define what we're changing Always first ›
Identify 3–5 candidate behaviours and align all stakeholders on a single specific, measurable target. Most projects fail here — not because change is impossible, but because every stakeholder is trying to change a different behaviour. We resolve that before any fieldwork begins.
A Behavioural Audit — Observe, map, understand Core diagnostic ›
360° field assessment: direct observation, FGD, interview, survey, and literature meta-analysis. We segment the population, map user personas using empathy frameworks, and produce a step-by-step Key Behaviour Map — showing what people actually do, not what they say they do.
L Leveraging & Lowering — Find the lever Diagnosis ›
Identify and rank the factors that help people perform the behaviour (Leveraging Factors) and those that block it (Lowering Factors). The priority factor — the one with the highest impact and tractability — becomes the intervention target. This prevents the most common mistake: designing the intervention around the wrong barrier.
I Insight Tools — Design the nudge Design ›
Select the behaviour change mechanism using MINDSPACE and EAST: Messenger, Incentive, Norms, Defaults, Salience, Priming, Affect, Commitment, or Ego. We design the intervention — messaging, choice architecture, environment redesign, digital nudge, or campaign — and state the testable hypothesis it rests on.
B Behavioural Experiment — Test before scale Validation ›
Run a controlled pilot — field RCT, A/B test, or quasi-experimental design — comparing control (no intervention) against treatment (the nudge). Sample size is calculated for adequate statistical power. Ethics protocol is applied where required. We never recommend scaling an untested intervention.
E Evidence — Measure and validate impact Measurement ›
Collect, clean, and analyse outcome data against the baseline established in the Audit phase. We apply appropriate statistical methods, test for significance, and produce a clear evidence report — structured for internal leadership, donor reporting, or academic publication depending on the engagement format.
R Reflection — Scale what works. Iterate what doesn't. Scale ›
If the experiment confirms the hypothesis: we deliver a fully documented scale-up playbook — ready for your implementation team, leadership, or donors. If the hypothesis fails: we return to K with a revised hypothesis. KALIBER is a cycle, not a project. Most breakthroughs come on the second or third pass.

🏆 IRSA-BKF Best Paper 2022 Field Experiment British Embassy Jakarta × East Java Government

A poster changes everything — visual priming increased handwashing by 30% during COVID-19

The campaign assumed information was the barrier. It wasn't. KALIBER audit revealed the real barrier was environmental friction: handwashing stations were not positioned at the actual decision moments. A single low-cost intervention — repositioning soap and adding a visual priming cue at the correct decision point — produced a 30% compliance increase, verified against a control group (n=1,648).

✓ 30% compliance uplift · Published: DOI 10.1111/asap.12329
Visual priming Controlled experiment n=1,648 Peer-reviewed

Key finding

Information deficit was not the barrier — environmental friction was. Positioning the priming cue at the exact decision moment (approaching the washroom) produced measurable behaviour change at negligible cost.

Method

  • Pre/post measurement with control group (n=1,648)
  • Online survey + direct observation
  • Visual priming and saliency as the intervention mechanism

Published output

Analyses of Social Issues and Public Policy, 2022. Cited by Wakil Gubernur Jawa Timur, Emil Dardak. DOI: 10.1111/asap.12329 ↗

Indonesia's stunting rate is 21.5% — yet only 7.4% of caregivers attend the parenting classes that would fix it

Only 7.4% of eligible caregivers attended BKB parenting classes — and fathers were almost entirely absent. KALIBER audit revealed the barriers were not logistical: childcare is socially coded as women's work, and youth found BKB irrelevant. Sixteen segment-specific nudge interventions were designed. The result: caregiver attendance intention jumped by 61% on average; among male caregivers — the hardest group to move — 41% uplift.

Full Engagement 4 Phases · 12 Weeks Jasa Raharja — National Road Safety

Indonesia's national road safety training tells drivers what to do. So why do fatal accidents keep happening?

The audit revealed drivers already know the rules. The real barriers are optimism bias ("accidents don't happen to me"), present bias (comfort over precaution), and social norms (non-compliance normalised by peers). The redesign applied EAST and COM-B to all 5 modules — replacing 812 words of technical instruction in the brake failure module with a three-step visual heuristic, reducing cognitive load at the exact conditions that cause accidents.

✓ 5 redesigned SKKNI-compliant modules · Behavioural validation completed
EAST framework COM-B diagnosis Cognitive load reduction 5 modules

4-phase structure

  • Phase 1 (Wk 1–3): Current State Assessment — gap analysis per user group across all 5 modules
  • Phase 2 (Wk 4–5): Behavioural Audit — score each module for clarity, salience, and nudge effectiveness using EAST + COM-B
  • Phase 3 (Wk 6–9): Validation — SKKNI alignment + survey comparing original vs redesigned for engagement and compliance motivation
  • Phase 4 (Wk 10–12): Presentation Materials — 5 redesigned decks with speaker notes

Key findings

  • Brake failure module: 812 words, 17 slides — extreme cognitive overload for truck and bus drivers
  • Dominant barrier: optimism bias and peer normalisation, not information deficit
  • Solution: commitment device (checklist) + narrative persona framing + social norm messaging

Consultation + Training Badan Pendapatan Daerah (BAPEDA) DKI Jakarta — Regional Revenue Agency

What if paying local taxes wasn't just about penalties — but about psychology?

Jakarta's tax compliance challenge is not about awareness — most Jakartans know they should pay. The barriers are social norm (others aren't paying either), process friction (forms are hard), and identity disconnect (paying feels like giving to an abstract institution, not to the city). Three nudge strategies were designed: descriptive social norm messaging, form simplification, and civic identity reframing. BAPEDA staff were trained to apply the KALIBER diagnostic independently.
